# Domain checklists

Read only the relevant section. These checks supplement, not replace, the main research protocol.

## 1. Politics and current news

- Separate allegation, confirmation, official position, and independent verification.
- Record both event date and publication date.
- Open the speech, bill, vote record, budget, filing, court document, or dataset behind coverage.
- Check corrections and updates on fast-moving stories.
- Distinguish legal authority, announced policy, proposed policy, and implemented policy.
- Compare reporting from genuinely independent organizations; wire copies are one origin.
- Treat polling as a measurement with sample, field dates, wording, weighting, and margin—not a fact about all voters.
- Avoid inferring an ideology's whole content from one politician or regime.

## 2. Economics and public policy

- Define nominal versus real, total versus per capita, stock versus flow, median versus mean.
- State currency, price base, purchasing-power adjustment, time window, and population.
- Check the denominator and counterfactual.
- Separate accounting identity, observed association, causal estimate, forecast, and value judgment.
- Inspect revisions, seasonality, base effects, and cherry-picked start dates.
- For policy claims, examine distribution: who benefits, who pays, when, and under which assumptions.
- Compare model predictions with realized outcomes only on equivalent definitions.

## 3. Science and health

- Prefer systematic reviews, high-quality trials, authoritative guidelines, and transparent datasets over a single study.
- Distinguish preprint, peer-reviewed paper, press release, and media summary.
- Report population, intervention, comparator, outcome, sample size, effect size, uncertainty, and study design.
- Distinguish relative from absolute risk.
- Check replication, retraction, expressions of concern, subgroup fishing, and conflicts of interest.
- Do not turn mechanistic plausibility or animal evidence into a proven human outcome.
- Separate absence of evidence from evidence of absence.
- Avoid personalized diagnosis or treatment instructions; advise qualified care for high-stakes individual decisions.

## 4. Law and regulation

- Identify jurisdiction, effective date, controlling text, and current status.
- Separate statute, regulation, guidance, court interpretation, proposal, and media description.
- Verify whether a decision is binding, appealed, stayed, superseded, or limited to specific facts.
- Quote operative language minimally and in context.
- Avoid universal legal conclusions from another jurisdiction.
- State when a licensed professional is needed for an individual case.

## 5. History and ideology

- Freeze definitions before comparing “communism,” “liberalism,” “democracy,” “capitalism,” “socialism,” “fascism,” or similar umbrella terms.
- Separate doctrine, party program, regime practice, wartime practice, later reinterpretation, and opponent caricature.
- Specify place and period; avoid treating one country or decade as the whole ideology.
- Use primary texts plus credible historiography. Do not let a quotation fragment stand in for a body of work.
- Distinguish descriptive history from moral evaluation.
- Check population estimates, archival uncertainty, retrospective propaganda, and translation choices.
- A counterexample defeats a universal claim, not necessarily a probabilistic trend.
- Refuting one regime narrative does not prove the opposite ideology.

## 6. Religion and philosophy

- Identify tradition, denomination, school, text edition, language, translation, and interpretive method.
- Separate “the text says,” “a school interprets,” “historically practiced,” and “morally justified.”
- Read the surrounding passage and relevant cross-references; do not quote-mine.
- Distinguish factual historical claims from theology and value judgments.
- Represent internal diversity fairly; one speaker does not define all adherents.
- Avoid contempt toward believers or nonbelievers. Challenge propositions and interpretations.
- When no shared premises exist, expose the premise difference instead of claiming a factual knockout.

## 7. Images, video, and social-media provenance

- Find the earliest available upload and full-length version.
- Compare frames before and after the viral segment.
- Inspect captions, subtitles, translation, audio continuity, cropping, speed, and aspect ratio.
- Use reverse-image or keyframe search when available.
- Check location, weather, landmarks, shadows, metadata, and independent contemporaneous footage when material.
- Distinguish edited, misleadingly captioned, synthetic, reenacted, and authentic-but-out-of-context media.
- Do not declare deepfake or authenticity from visual intuition alone.
- Preserve uncertainty if the original file or chain of custody is unavailable.
